# Laurel Oak Bloodstock — Decisions Log

_Running record of settled questions, newest last. Each entry: what was decided, why, and what it forces elsewhere. The model documents carry the resulting design; this file carries the reasoning._

---

## 2 Sep 2026 — Scope

**D1. The platform is a public site plus an owner portal, built around an end-to-end communication system.**
Replaces Relenta, MiStable and most email.

**D2. Statements, invoicing and payments are out of scope.**
Integration unlikely even in future. Keeps the boundary clean: this system handles communication and record, not money.

**D3. Events span the horse's whole life, not just race starts.**
Purchase, racing, care, breeding, selling, admin. They tie together on the horse — bought as a yearling, races, retires as a broodmare, her progeny sold.
*Forces:* the horse is the aggregate root, not the event.

---

## 2 Sep 2026 — Attribution

**D4. Four-tier attribution, with a permanent pending queue.**
Tier 1 routing token, tier 2 per-horse address, tier 3 heuristic match, tier 4 human triage. The queue is a designed screen, not an error state — an owner emailing out of the blue about raceday photos will never be attributable automatically.

**D5. Every horse gets a dedicated email address.**
Trainers are trained toward it. Gives certainty about the *horse* even when the event is ambiguous, so a trainer's voice note parks on the horse timeline rather than being guessed into the wrong race.

**D6. Racing body and club integrations are built ad-hoc as they arrive.**
Much may come via Arion, accessed as an ordinary third-party API consumer.

---

## 2 Sep 2026 — Ownership and access

**D7. Registered and managed ownership are two separate ledgers.**
The official record contains co-owners Laurel Oak must never contact. A registered line mapping to nothing internal is a permanent, legitimate state.
*Forces:* all communications and portal access derive from the managed book only. Race books and official displays use the registered record. They never mix.

**D8. Access follows the current interest, not the dates it was held.**
An exiting owner is locked out immediately, with no further communications. An incoming owner sees the horse's complete history back to purchase.
*Forces:* item scope becomes the only mechanism protecting confidentiality — see D9.

**D9. "Owners at the time" is a distinct item scope, and the default for anything generated by an ownership change.**
Exit negotiations, shares offered around, payment problems. Never visible to later owners. Staff opt out rather than opting in.

**D10. A returning horse's new ownership group does not inherit the previous management period.** _(default, pending confirmation)_

## 2 Sep 2026 — Lifecycle, visibility, data, media

**D11. Events close manually and can be reopened. Nothing closes automatically.**
A race event can run well past the day-after report — a celebration dinner a week after a win still belongs to that race. A closed event that receives a matching item reopens itself rather than rejecting it.
*Forces:* the matcher cannot rely on open/closed to pick a candidate. It weights by expected date, last activity and event type, and treats long-inactive events as dormant — surfaced as a nudge to close, de-weighted in matching.

**D12. Trainers never see owner replies. No exceptions.**
*Forces the most consequential technical constraint so far:* an outbound message may not address more than one audience class. A trainer and an owner are never on the same thread, never cc'd together, and a reply-all can never reach across. Routing tokens are issued **per recipient**, not per event — which also means a reply from an unrecognised address still identifies the sender exactly, strengthening tier 1 attribution.

**D13. Race and sale reference data is mirrored locally, not fetched live.**
Volume is small — around 300 race starters a year. Buys historical accuracy (conditions as at the day), resilience to feed outages, and fast queries, at negligible storage cost.

**D14. Media is hosted and retained, not treated as disposable attachments.**
Part of what replaces MiStable. Owners expect a gallery of their horse, not just files buried in a timeline.
*Forces:* a per-horse media library view alongside the stream; originals kept, with derived thumbnails and transcodes; audio and video transcribed.
